Life Story

William Richard “Dickey” Bell Sr. was born in Lancaster, South Carolina, on August 15, 1956, and died at home in Lancaster on July 1, 2026, age 69.

Dickey began working for Hendrix Furniture at about age 15. In 1977 he began full-time work with Springs Industries at the Grace Bleachery Plant, on third shift in screen print. He remained on third shift until entering the Production Management Program after his 1985 bachelor’s degree. He later became a Systems Analyst III, held several Customer Service Center roles, and finished his Springs career at the Fort Mill office.

He attended USC Lancaster part-time; his transfer record covers 1973–1981 with 26 hours earned and a 2.98 GPA. He graduated cum laude from Limestone College on May 4, 1985, with a Bachelor of Science in Business Administration/Management and a 3.654 GPA. He earned an MBA from Winthrop College on December 15, 1990. His graduate GPA is believed to have been approximately 3.9, possibly with one B, but that figure remains unconfirmed.

Dickey and Sonja grew up in the same neighborhood but only knew of each other until they met while attending Limestone. They began dating September 29, 1982, and married August 7, 1994, at Grace Presbyterian Church in Lancaster, officiated by Rev. William Dysard, Jr.

After their marriage they lived on Winsor Drive in Lancaster. They also owned a second home at Tilghman Shores, Unit B-8, North Myrtle Beach, from about August 1996 until May 15, 2008.

After Springs closed, Dickey continued his information-technology education. He earned the CompTIA Network+ certification on July 30, 2007, and a Network Administration certificate from York Technical College on August 10, 2007.

A 1998 Lancaster News feature on Technical Solutions listed Dickey as an owner. Family history clarifies that he was listed to help strengthen future loan and contract applications because of his education and experience; he was not actually an owner of the business.

Dickey worked at ArrowPointe Credit Union in IT beginning in 2007. He received the Support Staff of the Year Award, and his photograph appeared on the Spring 2016 cover of The Pointe. After Founders Federal Credit Union acquired ArrowPointe at the end of 2021, he continued at a Founders branch. His last working day was April 30, 2026; he remained employed at the time of his death.

Dickey was brilliant, a perfectionist, very personable, and had a smart-ass sense of humor. He enjoyed lawn care, his Mazda Miata convertible, and shooting. Shooting was primarily a father-and-son activity with Rickey. He was a devoted Clemson Tigers fan but attended at least three South Carolina Gamecocks games with Rickey.

In his final months Dickey suffered from advanced lung cancer. After hospital stays and treatment in Lancaster and Florence, further treatment was canceled and he entered hospice care. Sonja cared for him at home until his death.

In August 2026, Founders renamed its Spirit Award the Bell Spirit Award and began manufacturing a metal bell for outstanding employees in his honor.

Family & Home History

  • Mason Street: Before Fairview Drive, Alma, Buster, Dickey, and Spooky lived together on Mason Street in Lancaster, South Carolina.
  • Fairview Drive: Buster and Alma Bell owned the family home. Dickey moved there around 1967; Spooky also lived there. After Alma’s death in 1994, the property was sold.
  • Grace Avenue: Dickey, Debbie, and Rickey lived there from about March 22, 1976 until Dickey moved out around June 1978. Debbie and Rickey are believed to have remained there until the property sold May 22, 1979.
  • Confederate Avenue: co-owned by Dickey and Sonja. Sonja lived there before their marriage; Dickey never lived there. It was sold to Rickey in 1994.
  • Winsor Drive: Dickey and Sonja’s marital home from 1994 until Dickey’s death.
  • North Myrtle Beach: annual family vacations predated the Tilghman Shores purchase. Alma, Buster, Rickey, Heather, Dickey, and later Sonja vacationed there about a week each summer, staying in rental properties arranged through White Realty; Spooky and Helen joined most years. Dickey and Sonja later purchased Tilghman Shores Unit B-8 around August 1996.
  • Rickey’s childhood: primarily co-parented by Dickey, Alma, and Buster, with Sundays and some Saturday nights spent with maternal grandparents Peggy and Ebb Faulkenberry.

Family Memories

The Fire-Ant Shooting Trip

Dickey and Rickey often went shooting together in the 1980s. They would stop on the way to the gravel pits and pick up cans and other safe targets. On one trip Heather came along and stepped from an older Toyota 4WD truck directly into a fire-ant mound. Dickey tackled her and beat the ants off her legs; both were bitten badly.

North Myrtle Beach

The early beach photographs belong to the annual family vacations and were taken before Dickey and Sonja purchased their Tilghman Shores second home.

The Buck Stove — Early 1980s

Dad was in his wild-child mode in the early 1980s. One night he didn't come home, which wasn't particularly unusual at the time.

Granddad Buster was mad because Dad was supposed to help cut wood for the Buck stove in the living room. He called Dad's friend Donald Barrett looking for him. Donald said Dad was there, asleep, and would not get up.

Buster told Donald, in not-polite terms, to throw water on his A** and wake him up; he had stuff to do!

Family recollection — Rickey Bell
